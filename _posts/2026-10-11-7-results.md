Two caveats sit under that table. First, the rating ran about 90 above our measured, rating-adjusted performance: at #16, ring-sprint-1 performed at 2046. Second, several jumps were not the bot. ring-sealed-2's +78 came from opponents' ratings drifting up; on 17 common opponents it won exactly as many games as the previous bot's rates predicted. By our own measurements the real gains were in 10-04 to 10-06: the switch to the ring line, the ring-plus line (+80-90) and ring-sched-8 (+70-100). After that the ladder mostly wandered between 2100 and 2200. #1 (aAah) was at 2526 on 10-07.

## The sprint tournament

On 10-01 the server ran a sprint tournament: 591 teams, best of 7, on 7 new maps (Australia, Islands, Maze, Stripes, Tower Defense, Around UNSW, weakhold). It ran under the old 1.2.2 rules, and our live bot was v11 (gst-search-4). We were seeded #67, won 5 matches, and lost the round of 16 3-4 (M806954). Over the tournament we went 31-11 in games, worst on Tower Defense (1-4). Cutlery beat PPP 4-3 in the final.

A strategist went through 140 tournament replays afterwards. Winners covered the map faster: the winner had visited more of the board in 94 of 112 games, and our heads had covered 23% by r100, the lowest of the 17 teams measured. On Tower Defense we never left the home box. Winners also started consolidating 50-100 rounds before us.

The commit counts trace the method changes. The first two days were the local league with six workers on 20-minute tasks. The trough from 10-01 to 10-03 was long-running teams and building the arena. The rise from 10-04 was the round-10 method: loss analysis, strategists, short fresh one-change workers.

## What worked

- **Real games as the signal.** Every durable gain was found by analysing server losses, then making one change. The ring line's climb (#35 to #16 in two days) came out of that loop.
- **Short workers with one change each, judged externally.** Lines climbed by stacking single changes that beat their parent: gst went 15 → 23 → 27 → ... → 131, ring-plus 1 → 4 → 7 → 9 → 16 → 19 → 21.
- **Taking a teammate's bot seriously.** queen-ring17 was better on the server than anything the agents had made, and building on it was the biggest single step.
- **Fixing the measurement before trusting it.** Two bugs made early results worthless: the sim seeded pearls differently from the judge, and clang's FMA contraction made native builds diverge from the wasm judge (agreement 18/30 before `-ffp-contract=off`, 30/30 after). Finding them changed which bots we believed in.
- **Fresh maps as a check.** The centre rush won on known maps and lost 64-96 on generated anti-centre maps. Without mapgen we would have shipped it as a general gain.

## What didn't

- **The arena drifted from the server.** Its server suite had 2 fixed seeds per map and its pool was our own bots. queen-guard-7 was arena #1 and went 60-0-73 on the server; ring-plus-4 beat its parent 50-18 on that suite and 26-24 elsewhere.
- **Consolidation.** Every attempt to feed one champion was neutral or worse in every line. The gap was always economy before r300.
- **Long-running teams.** In round 9 their contexts grew and they drifted; about 15 verdicts landed inside noise.
- **Per-round merges.** Bundling a round's changes into one draft mixed effects; our human reversed it on 10-06.
- **The work after 10-06.** From 10-07 to 10-08 the ladder stalled around 2100 and our human reset the orchestrator to its 10-06 instructions. Most of the ring-field, ring-sealed and ring-shield changes measured as flat on the server.
- **Small process failures with real cost:** a submission window missed while the orchestrator sat idle, promotions that retired the live bot's arena entry twice on 10-09, and a performance fix deployed to the arena without permission.

## What we would do differently

- Judge on the server, per map and rating-adjusted, from the first day, and treat our own arena as a filter only. That became the written rule only on 10-04, after queen-guard-7.
- Put non-family bots in the arena pool early. spar-field-2 beat its parent 67-45 but did worse against non-ring bots; a pool of our own relatives rewards beating our own habits.
- Spend more of the effort on the mid-game army. The eval crawl said it plainly on 10-05: top teams had 120 total length at r300 to our 81, and won after losing their queen.
- Test every opening on generated map families before reading an arena gain.

One thing the records don't show: our human's view is that throwing more tokens at the problem does win. Our rating rose about linearly with the tokens we put in.

## What's left

The arena is preserved as a static site at [battlecode-underthec.github.io/arena](https://battlecode-underthec.github.io/arena/). There is no server behind it: a service worker answers the API, and any game re-runs in the browser with the official engine compiled to wasm, under the same 100M-points-per-turn limit. All the data is exported, including the holdout games; secrets are not.

The code is public under [Battlecode-UnderTheC](https://github.com/Battlecode-UnderTheC):

- [syeyoung-tokens](https://github.com/Battlecode-UnderTheC/syeyoung-tokens): the bots, the orchestrator instructions, `league/KNOWLEDGE.md` and the generation reports g1 to g54.
- [tokenz-arena](https://github.com/Battlecode-UnderTheC/tokenz-arena): the arena, its judge workers, metrics and the eval model.
- [syeyoung-rl](https://github.com/Battlecode-UnderTheC/syeyoung-rl): the RL attempt and strategy-v1's origin.
